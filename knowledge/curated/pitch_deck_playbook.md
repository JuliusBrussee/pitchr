# Pitch Deck Playbook

Synthesized from YC, Sequoia, Slidebean, OpenVC, and top-funded deck teardowns.

## Core Philosophy

1. **Intrigue enough for a meeting.** The deck is not the pitch — it earns a 30-minute conversation. Every slide must make the reader want to see the next one.
2. **Say what you do like baby food.** If a smart 12-year-old cannot explain your company after slide 2, rewrite it. Kill jargon, kill abstraction.
3. **Story first, then slides.** The narrative arc matters more than any single slide. Each slide must hand off to the next with a clear "and therefore..." connector.
4. **Founder voice, not consultant voice.** Write like a confident founder talking to a partner at dinner, not a McKinsey deck. Bold claims backed by evidence.

## Narrative Arc (8 Slides)

### 1. Hook
Open with the boldest true claim about your company. Not "Welcome to CompanyX" — instead, the single sentence that makes a partner stop scrolling. Company name is secondary context, not the headline.

### 2. Problem
Quantify the pain. Name who feels it. Show why existing alternatives fail. Use bottom-up math to size the problem in dollars. The reader must feel "this is broken and worth fixing."

### 3. Solution
Show the mechanism: how specifically your product solves the problem. One clear before/after. Include early evidence — a quote, a pilot result, a screenshot metric. Do NOT just describe features.

### 4. Traction (before Market — proof before claims)
Lead with the hardest metric you have: revenue, users, growth rate, retention. Show trajectory, not snapshots. If pre-revenue, show LOIs, waitlist signups, or pilot results. Traction validates everything that follows.

### 5. Market + Competition (merged — one view)
Bottom-up TAM: start from unit economics and multiply by reachable customers. Never quote a Gartner number without grounding it. Name 2-3 real competitors, state what they do well, then state your specific advantage. Avoid "no competition" — it signals naivete.

### 6. Business Model
Own one simple model: "We charge $X per Y." Show unit economics (CAC, LTV, payback). If you have a wedge-and-expand strategy, show it. Keep it to one revenue model — complexity kills trust.

### 7. Team
Founders only. No advisors, no "team of 47." For each founder: name, one-line credential that directly relates to why they win in this market. Previous exits, domain expertise, or technical depth — pick the strongest signal.

### 8. Ask
State the exact amount. Tie use-of-funds to milestones ("$X gets us to Y milestone"). Show what the company looks like 18 months post-raise. Make the investor see the next fundraise headline.

## Content Standards

- **Bottom-up sizing:** Start from your unit price x addressable customer count. Top-down TAM without proof is noise.
- **Own one model:** "We charge $X per Y" with clear unit economics. If you cannot explain pricing in one sentence, simplify it.
- **Concrete ask:** "$2.5M to reach 500 paying customers by Q4 2025" not "raising a round."
- **No orphan claims:** Every assertion must connect to evidence on the same slide or the previous one.
- **Stage-aware emphasis:**
  - Pre-seed: Problem clarity, founder-market fit, early signal (waitlist, LOIs)
  - Seed: Working product, initial traction, clear GTM wedge
  - Series A: Revenue trajectory, unit economics, path to scale

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do These)

1. **Placeholder text:** Never use `[placeholder]`, `[TBD]`, `[insert X]`. Three alternatives when data is missing:
   - Extrapolate with framing: "~$2M ARR based on current MoM growth rate"
   - Directional language: "Growing 15-20% month-over-month since launch"
   - Omit entirely: if you cannot say something concrete, leave it out
2. **Narrative drift:** Each slide must connect to the previous one. If you cannot say "and therefore..." between two consecutive slides, fix the order or rewrite.
3. **TAM without proof:** "$50B market (Grand View Research)" without showing how you reach even $10M of it is worthless. Always show the bottom-up path.
4. **Buzzword headlines:** "Innovative AI-Powered Platform" tells investors nothing. Headlines must make specific, falsifiable claims: "We Cut Returns 34% for DTC Brands."
5. **Vague asks:** "Raising a seed round" is not an ask. "$1.5M to ship v2 and reach 100 paying customers" is an ask.
6. **Fake avatars / placeholder visuals:** Single-letter circles as team photos look amateur. Use clean text rows instead.
7. **Feature lists without outcomes:** "Real-time dashboard, API integrations, ML pipeline" — so what? Show what changes for the customer.
