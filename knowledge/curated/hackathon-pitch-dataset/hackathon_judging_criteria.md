# Hackathon Judging Criteria — Compiled Reference

## ETHGlobal
- **Practicality** — Does it solve a real problem? Could someone use it today?
- **Usability** — Is the UX intuitive? Can a non-technical person navigate it?
- **WOW Factor** — Is it creative, surprising, or technically impressive?

## MLH (Major League Hacking)
- **Awesomeness** — Does the project inspire excitement and curiosity?
- **Technical Aptitude** — How technically complex is the implementation?
- **Originality** — Is the idea novel? Does it offer a fresh perspective?
- **Usefulness** — Does the project solve a meaningful problem?

## NASA Space Apps Challenge
- **Creativity** — Is the approach original? Does it reframe the problem?
- **Impact** — How significant is the potential real-world effect?
- **Implementation** — How complete and functional is the solution?
- **Presentation** — Is the pitch clear, compelling, and well-structured?

## Devpost / General Hackathon
- **Innovation** — Is the idea original or does it improve on existing solutions?
- **Technical Complexity** — Does the project demonstrate advanced technical skills?
- **Design** — Is the product well-designed and user-friendly?
- **Completion** — How close is the project to a working product?
- **Learning** — Did the team learn new skills or technologies?

## Hack the North
- **Technical Difficulty** — Complexity of implementation
- **Polish** — Quality of the finished product
- **Creativity** — Uniqueness of the idea and approach
- **Usefulness** — Practical application and impact

## LA Hacks
- **Innovation** — Novelty of the solution
- **Technical Achievement** — Depth of implementation
- **Design & Usability** — User experience quality
- **Presentation** — Quality of the demo pitch
- **Impact** — Potential real-world benefit

## Common Patterns Across All Criteria
1. **Working demo** is weighted heavily at every event — slides alone never win.
2. **Creativity/originality** appears in every rubric — novel framing matters.
3. **Technical depth** is expected but must be balanced with usability.
4. **Real-world impact** separates winners from technically impressive but impractical hacks.
5. **Presentation quality** is a multiplier — the best hack loses if poorly demoed.
