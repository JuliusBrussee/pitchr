# Deck Commonality Notes

## Shared DNA / Stage-by-Stage (verbatim)

Shared DNA across strong fundraising pitches:
- Lead with immediate clarity: what you do, for whom, and why now.
- Make the problem economically explicit: who pays for pain and what it costs.
- Show mechanism before magic: explain how the product creates the outcome.
- Replace adjectives with proof: named metrics, timeframe, and denominator.
- Demonstrate wedge first: specific beachhead, then expansion path.
- Differentiate against real alternatives, not a generic "status quo" strawman.
- Close with a concrete ask tied to milestone outputs and timeline.

Stage-by-stage emphasis:
- Pre-seed: founder insight, problem urgency, prototype/pilot proof, narrow wedge.
- Seed: repeatable acquisition or retention signal, clearer GTM loop, explicit 12-18 month ask outcomes.
- Series A: growth mechanics + unit economics coherence, defensibility under competition, execution bench.
- Series B: operating leverage, category strategy durability, expansion economics and governance discipline.

## Canonical Deck Flow (V1)

- Hook: one-line statement of what the company does and why now.
- Problem: clear pain with urgency and audience specificity.
- Solution: product mechanism with proof of capability.
- Traction: concrete metrics with time windows.
- Market and competition: TAM/SAM plus direct alternatives and wedge.
- GTM and moat: distribution logic and defensibility.
- Team and execution: founder-market fit and hiring plan.
- Ask and milestones: amount, runway, and measurable outcomes.

## Common Failure Modes

- Narrative drift across slides.
- TAM-heavy claims without direct customer proof.
- Vague ask not tied to milestones.
- Slide density that weakens signal clarity.
